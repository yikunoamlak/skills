# Moments of Impact

Identifying your **Moment of Impact** involves pinpointing the specific, high-stakes situations or actions that have the greatest influence on your success within a chosen "Field of Play." These moments represent the intersection where your **Action Layer** (behaviors and skills) meets the **Field of Play Layer** (the external environment and expectations).

## 1. Use Target Mapping (Working Backward)

Start by defining your final goal or desired outcome and work backward to see the steps required to reach it.

### The Needle-Mover
- Ask yourself: "What would truly move the needle on my career/goal?"
- Identify the one action that, if done consistently, would transform your results

### High Rate of Return
Look for activities that provide the highest percentage of return on your effort.
- **Example:** A sales executive identified that while he performed many tasks, "lunch-and-learn" sessions were the specific activity that most effectively created new business opportunities
- **Example:** A writer realized that the first 500 words each morning had more impact than 3 hours of afternoon editing

## 2. Locate the Resistance

Moments of Impact are typically the areas of your life filled with the **most resistance, emotion, and challenge**.

### Vulnerability
These moments occur when you feel most vulnerable to judgment from others.
- Public speaking
- Asking for what you're worth
- Setting a boundary
- Admitting you don't know something

### The "Hiding" Spot
Look for where you tend to "hide" or shrink:
- For an athlete: "crunch time" in a game
- For a writer: the moment they sit down to put words on paper
- For a salesperson: the follow-up call after a rejection
- For a leader: the difficult conversation they keep postponing

## 3. Identify Where the "Trapped Self" Shows Up

Analyze the areas of your Field of Play where you feel discouraged or frustrated because you aren't performing to your full capability.

### Inconsistency
- Pinpoint moments where you sometimes bring your "Heroic Self" but not often enough to achieve your desired results
- These are areas where your performance is unreliable—not because of lack of skill, but because of emotional interference

### Self-Sabotage
- Identify where the "Enemy" (inner critic, fear, or imposter syndrome) stands guard to prevent you from taking action
- Often appears just before the biggest opportunities

## Common Examples for Inspiration

If you are struggling to name yours, consider these common Moments of Impact:

### Business
- Closing a sale
- Negotiating contracts
- Pitching to investors
- Delivering bad news
- Asking for a raise

### Public Life
- Delivering a presentation
- Networking at an event
- Speaking up in a team meeting
- Introducing yourself to a stranger
- Handling a Q&A session

### Sports
- Taking the final shot
- Battling for the puck in a corner
- Serving for the match
- Stepping up to the free-throw line
- Entering the arena

### Personal Life
- Apologizing
- Saying "I love you"
- Setting a boundary with family
- Making the decision to invest your money
- Asking for help

## The Moment of Impact Diagnostic

Once you identify this moment, you can build a specific **Alter Ego** designed with the "Superpowers" necessary to win in that precise environment.

### Questions to Identify Your Moment of Impact:
1. Where do I feel the most resistance?
2. What action, if done well, would change everything?
3. Where do I consistently underperform despite having the skills?
4. What situation makes my stomach drop just thinking about it?
5. If I could show up as my most powerful self in one area, where would it be?
