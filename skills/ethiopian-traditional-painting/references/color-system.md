# Color System — Ethiopian Traditional Painting

## Primary Palette

These are the ONLY colors used in Ethiopian traditional painting. The power of the style comes from its restrained, harmonious color language.

### Warm Ivory — Background & Ground
- **Hex**: #F5E6C8
- **RGB**: 245, 230, 200
- **Usage**: Primary background color, skin highlights, garment base
- **Rules**: Always the dominant color by area. Never pure white (#FFFFFF). Never dark.

### Deep Red — Sacrifice & Divinity
- **Hex**: #8B1A1A
- **RGB**: 139, 26, 26
- **Usage**: Panel borders, garment accents, ceremonial objects, lips
- **Rules**: Used for emphasis and framing. Not for large fills. Always paired with warm ivory.

### Terracotta — Earth & Architecture
- **Hex**: #C65D3B
- **RGB**: 198, 93, 59
- **Usage**: Borders, architectural elements, skin undertones, ground
- **Rules**: Secondary framing color. Warmer and lighter than deep red.

### Golden Ochre — Royalty & Light
- **Hex**: #D4A017
- **RGB**: 212, 160, 23
- **Usage**: Garment details, halos, crowns, decorative patterns, borders
- **Rules**: Represents holiness and wealth. Sparingly on garments, generously on halos.

### Forest Green — Life & Fertility
- **Hex**: #2D5A27
- **RGB**: 45, 90, 39
- **Usage**: Garment details, trees, plants, border accents
- **Rules**: Represents Ethiopia and growth. Used in textiles and nature elements.

### Warm Brown — Skin & Earth
- **Hex**: #6B3A2A
- **RGB**: 107, 58, 42
- **Usage**: Skin tones, hair, outlines, earth, shadows
- **Rules**: Primary figure color. Mix with warm ivory for skin variation.

### Muted Blue — Water & Sky (Sparingly)
- **Hex**: #3A5A7C
- **RGB**: 58, 90, 124
- **Usage**: Garment details, water, sky accents (very limited)
- **Rules**: Use only when specifically needed. Never dominate.

### Black — Outlines & Definition
- **Hex**: #1A1A1A
- **RGB**: 26, 26, 26
- **Usage**: All outlines, hair, eyes, text
- **Rules**: The structural backbone. Every form is defined by black outline.

### White — Garments & Highlights
- **Hex**: #FFFFFF
- **RGB**: 255, 255, 255
- **Usage**: Garment base (habesha kemis), eye whites, cloth
- **Rules**: Only for specific white garments and eye details. Never for background.

## Color Mixing Rules

### Skin Tones
Base: Warm Brown (#6B3A2A) + Warm Ivory (#F5E6C8)
- Light skin: More ivory
- Medium skin: Equal mix
- Dark skin: More brown, add touch of deep red

### Garment Colors
Base: White (#FFFFFF) + one accent color
- Border patterns: Deep red, golden ochre, forest green
- Embroidery: Geometric patterns in accent colors

### Background
Always: Warm Ivory (#F5E6C8)
- Never gradient
- Never pure white
- Never dark

## Color Relationships

### Complementary Pairs (within palette)
- Deep Red + Forest Green — strongest contrast, use for emphasis
- Golden Ochre + Muted Blue — regal pairing, use for royalty

### Analogous Harmony
- Deep Red → Terracotta → Golden Ochra — warm progression
- Forest Green → Muted Blue — cool accent

### Neutral Foundation
- Warm Ivory + Warm Brown + Black — the base layer
- Everything sits on top of this

## Forbidden Colors

Never use any of these:
- Pure black backgrounds
- Neon or fluorescent colors
- Gradients or blended colors
- Purple (not in traditional palette)
- Pink (not in traditional palette)
- Orange (use terracotta instead)
- Gray (use warm ivory or brown instead)
- Metallic or shiny effects

## Color Application by Subject

### Religious Figures
- Golden halos and crowns
- White garments with red/green/gold borders
- Warm brown skin

### Royal Figures
- Elaborate golden garments
- Colorful ceremonial umbrellas
- Rich patterned textiles

### Common People
- White habesha kemis
- Simple colored borders
- Natural skin tones

### Nature
- Forest green for foliage
- Warm brown for trunks and earth
- Warm ivory for sky

### Architecture
- Terracotta for walls
- Warm brown for roofs
- Black for outlines and doors
