# Worked Examples

Two briefs carried from discovery to plan. Use them to calibrate depth, not to copy answers.

---

## Example 1 - Build mode: a landing page for a water-monitoring startup

**Discovery, compressed**

- **Tell:** farmers should believe an affordable sensor can warn them before a leak ruins a crop.
- **Audience:** 30-50 hectare growers in Kenya and Ethiopia; already burned by one expensive gadget; sceptical of SaaS language.
- **Making:** a one-page product site, in English and Amharic, launching in six weeks. Draft headline: "AI-powered irrigation intelligence platform".
- **Impact:** request a demo. Success = demo requests from growers, not just distributors.
- **Stakes:** one ruined season can end a farm. **Conflict:** cost and scepticism versus the risk of doing nothing.
- **Transformation:** from finding out too late to knowing in time.
- **Evidence:** one grower's story with dates, two field photos, leak-detection accuracy figure.
- **Feeling:** relief. **Anti-reference:** anything that looks like a Silicon Valley dashboard pitch.

**Toolkit (8):** 88 Trigger or Wicked Problem (spine), 91 The Quarter-Inch Hole (spine), 73 Show, Don't Tell (spine), 3 Analogy (spine), 96 Verisimilitude, 7 Calls to Action, 86 The Peak-End Rule, 35 Hofstede's Cultural Dimensions.

**Arc:** 88 the leak that already happened (in media res) -> 91 what the grower is actually hiring (a warning, not a dashboard) -> 3 explained in one familiar comparison -> 73 the scene at 5 a.m. when the alert arrives -> 96 proof with numbers and a face -> 7 one action: request a demo.

**Edits (top three)**

1. Headline: "Know about the leak the same morning, not the week after the crop fails." Sub: one sentence naming the alert and the response time.
2. Replace the dashboard hero image with the alert screen as a farmer receives it, timestamped, plus the field photo it refers to.
3. Move the accuracy figure directly under the first claim, with the measurement period stated.

**Cut:** the phrase "AI-powered irrigation intelligence platform"; three feature icons above the fold; the second CTA.

**Test:** show the page for five seconds to two growers; ask them to say what it does.

## Example 2 - Review mode: an annual report for a health NGO

**What was read:** a 24-page PDF, reports-first, opening with a letter from the chair, closing with a financial appendix.

**Ranked findings (6 of 12 kept)**

1. **No conflict (11, 88).** The report describes activity without a problem it is solving. Fix: open on one patient's situation and the obstacle, then let the numbers answer it.
2. **Beginning equals end (12).** Page 1 and page 24 are both institutional voice. Fix: engineer a visible state change; close on what is different now.
3. **Telling, not showing (73).** "We improved access to maternal care" with no scene. Fix: one worked example with a place, a person, and a date.
4. **Weak peak (86).** The most important result sits mid-document among equal-weight charts. Fix: stage it as the climax, strip competing exhibits around it.
5. **Numbers without a protagonist (15).** Twelve charts, none annotated to a question. Fix: one chart, annotated, answering one question, with the action beneath it.
6. **Ending is an appendix (7).** The report stops instead of asking. Fix: a closing page with one specific next step for the reader.

**Not flagged:** visual design, tone, and accessibility passed; no claims were found to be unevidenced (26).

**Next test:** show pages 1, 12, and 24 only, and ask a donor to summarise the year.
