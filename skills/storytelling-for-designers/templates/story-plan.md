## 4. The toolkit
6-15 principles, each earning its place.

| # | Principle | Why it fits this brief | The change it forces |
|---|-----------|------------------------|----------------------|

Group: **spine** (the 4-6 that define the story) and **situational** (the rest, only where the brief needs them).

## 6. What to cut
The elements that dilute the story, each with the principle that condemns it.

## 7. QA checklist
Tick before calling it done. See `SKILL.md` for the full list.

- [ ] A stranger can state the message after five seconds
- [ ] The beginning and the end are clearly different states
- [ ] There is a conflict or stake, not just a description
- [ ] At least one moment shows rather than tells
- [ ] The protagonist is the audience or their client
- [ ] The peak lands where memory concentrates
- [ ] One clear next step
- [ ] Claims are evidenced; nothing invented
- [ ] Jargon and clutter removed
- [ ] Works for the actual reader's culture, language, and access needs

## 8. Next test
The single cheapest check that would prove or disprove the plan, and who to run it on.
