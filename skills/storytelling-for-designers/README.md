# Storytelling for Designers

An interview-driven skill that turns what you want to say into a design that says it, using the 100 principles from *Universal Principles of Storytelling for Designers: 100 Key Concepts for Bringing Good Ideas to Life* by Lyle H. Sandler (Rockport Books).

You describe what you want to tell, who it is for, and what should happen. The skill extracts the rest, confirms a short brief, then hands back a tailored toolkit of principles - chosen for your situation, not all 100 - with a narrative arc mapped onto your artifact, concrete edits, what to cut, and a QA checklist.

## What it does

1. **Discover** - asks what you want to tell, who it is for, what you are making, and what should happen next, then fills the gaps: stakes, conflict, transformation, protagonist, evidence, emotion target, constraints.
2. **Confirm** - returns a compressed story brief and asks for one confirmation.
3. **Select** - picks a 4-6 principle spine plus situational entries from one of 15 need-based playbooks, capped at 15 principles, always spanning at least three of the book's five categories.
4. **Deliver** - a through-line, an arc mapped beat by beat onto your artifact, a toolkit table, numbered edits with real wording, a cut list, and the cheapest next test.
5. **Check** - a QA list: message legible in five seconds, visible contrast between start and end, a real conflict, a moment that shows rather than tells, the audience as protagonist, a staged peak, one clear next step.

Works in three modes: **Build** (nothing exists yet), **Shape** (a draft exists), **Review** (diagnose a finished artifact and rank findings by impact).

## When to use it

- "I want this page / deck / brand / campaign / report to tell a story"
- "How do I explain this so people get it?"
- "How do I make this data land?"
- "What story should my design be telling?"
- "Review my design for narrative problems"
- Pitching, origin and about stories, cause campaigns, service journeys, product UX writing, presentations, motion and video.
