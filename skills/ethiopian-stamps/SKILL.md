# Ethiopian Stamp Art

Create artwork that reads instantly as an Ethiopian postage stamp. Keep the frame,
typography, paper, and printing language authentic. The subject can be anything,
including a modern joke.

## What This Skill Produces

- A single stamp, vertical or horizontal.
- A se-tenant pair, strip, or block of four.
- A souvenir sheet or miniature sheet with a wide margin and title.
- A first-day cover (FDC) with stamp, cancellation, and cachet.
- A Pinterest pin (2:3) presenting one or more stamps on cream paper.

## Start With Five Decisions

1. **Era.** 1894 first issue, 1909-1935 overprints and early definitives, 1941-1974 imperial commemoratives, 1974-1991 Derg, 1991-now contemporary, or a modern concept stamp in a historical frame.
2. **Subject.** One clear subject from the subject bank below. Specific beats generic: a gelada, not "African wildlife".
3. **Format.** Single stamp, pair, sheet, first-day cover, or Pinterest pin.
4. **Text.** Country name, denomination, year, and at most one short slogan. Keep it to four strings.
5. **Humour.** None, gentle, or full modern gag. The frame stays serious; the subject carries the joke.

## The Stamp Contract

Every output must satisfy these. If one is missing, the image reads as a poster, a banknote, or a generic vintage label.

1. **A physical stamp, not a poster.** Show a piece of perforated paper with a printed design. On a pin or card, give it a cream margin and a soft shadow.
2. **Perforations.** A clean toothed edge around the whole stamp. Perforation 11.5 to 14.5, usually 14. The teeth should be regular and complete.
3. **Inner frame.** A thin rule, with an ornamental or engraved border for historical eras. The border is part of the design, not a picture frame.
4. **Amharic country name at the top.** Use `ኢትዮጵያ` (Ethiopia). Early 20th-century issues may instead read `POSTES ETHIOPIENNES`.
5. **One central vignette.** A portrait in a medallion, an animal, a landscape, an emblem, or an event. One focal point, not a collage.
6. **Latin country name at the bottom.** `ETHIOPIA`, or `ETHIOPIE` for the 1919-1930s look. Letterspaced capitals.
7. **Denomination.** Lower left or bottom centre. Match the era: guerche, cents, or birr. Include the currency word or symbol.
8. **Year.** Lower right or under the vignette. Latin numerals for modern concept stamps. Ge'ez numerals are optional for historical looks.
9. **Bilingual balance.** Amharic and Latin text sit in separate bands. Never bury one language inside the illustration.
10. **Cancellation is optional.** A circular postmark adds authenticity, but keep it off the face and off the denomination.
11. **Flat printing language.** Engraved lines, lithographic ink, offset colour. No glossy 3D, no plastic wrap, no digital gradient mesh.
12. **Label concept art.** If the stamp is not a real postal issue, say so in the pin caption or README. Never present fantasy as a real issue.

## Era Selector

| Era | Years | Visual language | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| First issue | 1894-1900 | Intaglio engraving, acanthus border, profile portrait or Lion of Judah, Amharic-only inscription, guerche values, blue/maroon/green on cream | Menelik-era portraits, lions, crosses, early postal history |
| Early definitives | 1901-1935 | `POSTES ETHIOPIENNES`, `ETHIOPIE`, Zewditu and Tafari portraits, native animals, coronation overprints, four-colour lithography | Regal portraits, heraldry, animal studies, overprint puzzles |
| Imperial commemoratives | 1941-1974 | Oval Haile Selassie portrait, photogravure, cents and dollars, globe and airmail motifs, wildlife, Olympics, Ethiopian Airlines | Airmail, achievements, wildlife, sports, state visits |
| Derg | 1974-1991 | Red, yellow, and green, workers and soldiers, revolution anniversaries, flat bold graphics, Amharic slogans | Bold poster energy, labour, land, development |
| Contemporary | 1991-now | Offset lithography, flat vector or photographic, birr and santim, tourism, coffee, UNESCO sites, sports, leaders | Modern commemoratives, culture, nature, national pride |
| Modern concept | Any | Take one historical frame and drop in a contemporary subject | Humour, internet culture, work, food, pets |

## Format Selector

| Format | Shape | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Single stamp | Vertical 2:3 to 3:4, or horizontal 3:2 | The default. One vignette, one message |
| Se-tenant pair | Two stamps joined | Two related subjects or a before/after; perforation runs through the middle |
| Strip or block | Three or four stamps | A mini-series; keep the frame identical and change the vignette |
| Souvenir sheet | Wide margin around one or four stamps | Margin carries the title, year, and a small motif. Use a large Amharic title and a Latin subtitle |
| First-day cover | Horizontal envelope | Stamp in the corner, circular first-day cancellation, illustrated cachet on the left |
| Pinterest pin | 2:3 vertical, 1440x2160 or 1696x2528 | Stamp centred at 55-70% of the height; cream paper; optional caption below; 120 px safe margin |

## Subject Bank

**Imperial and historical.** Menelik II, Zewditu, Haile Selassie I, Tafari Makonnen, Crown of Solomon, Lion of Judah with flag and cross, imperial throne, palace gates, coronation monument.

**Faith and heritage.** Ethiopian Orthodox processional crosses, Lalibela rock-hewn churches, Axum stelae, Ark of the Covenant, Timkat, Meskel, manuscript illumination, Ge'ez script.

**Land and nature.** Walia ibex, gelada, Ethiopian wolf, Menelik's bushbuck, Abyssinian hare, thick-billed raven, yellow-fronted parrot, coffee plant, teff, enset, juniper, Simien Mountains, Blue Nile Falls, Danakil, Rift Valley lakes.

**Culture and daily life.** Jebena coffee ceremony, mesob and injera, habesha kemis and netela with tilet borders, krar and masenqo, market scenes, horse and mule caravans, melektegnas (couriers with letters on sticks).

**Modern Ethiopia.** Ethiopian Airlines, Addis Ababa light rail, Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam, satellites, universities, hospitals, tech workers, marathon runners, African Union, United Nations.

**Humour subjects that work.** A cat as a crowned emperor, a gelada working from a laptop, a robot pouring coffee, injera as a museum treasure, a lion courier on a scooter, a goat with a smartphone, a walia ibex in a business suit. Keep the joke affectionate and put it in the vignette, never in the sacred symbols.

## Typography and Language

Use one Amharic font and one Latin font. Recommended: Noto Serif Ethiopic, Abyssinica SIL, Nyala, or Ethiopic Abay for Amharic; a Didone or Clarendon-style serif for historical Latin; a clean grotesque or transitional serif for modern Latin.

| Text | Amharic | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Ethiopia | ኢትዮጵያ | Top band, always |
| Ethiopian Postal Service | የኢትዮጵያ ፖስታ አገልግሎት | Souvenir sheets and FDCs |
| Post / mail | ፖስታ | Small labels, cancellations |
| Stamp | ቴምብር | Captions, FDCs |
| Birr | ብር | Denomination after 1976 |
| Santim | ሳንቲም | Small denominations |
| Coffee | ቡና | Coffee subjects |
| Coffee ceremony | የቡና ሥርዓት | Coffee ceremony stamps |
| Injera | እንጀራ | Food subjects |
| Lion of Judah | የይሁዳ አንበሳ | Imperial subjects |
| Peace | ሰላም | Slogans |
| Ethiopia | ETHIOPIA | Bottom band, letterspaced |

**Ge'ez numerals.** ፩ 1, ፪ 2, ፫ 3, ፬ 4, ፭ 5, ፮ 6, ፯ 7, ፰ 8, ፱ 9, ፲ 10, ፳ 20, ፴ 30, ፵ 40, ፶ 50, ፷ 60, ፸ 70, ፹ 80, ፺ 90, ፻ 100. Use them for historical denominations and years; pair them with Latin numerals on modern stamps.

**Placement rules.**

- Country name: top band, cap height 4-8% of stamp height.
- Denomination: lower left or bottom centre, cap height 6-10%.
- Year: lower right, cap height 3-5%.
- Slogan or subject label: one line above the bottom band, never a paragraph.
- Letterspace `ETHIOPIA` and `POSTES ETHIOPIENNES`; do not letterspace Amharic.

**Text safety.** Image models produce plausible but incorrect Ge'ez. Use only short, verified strings. If the generated Amharic is wrong, regenerate with fewer words, or crop the vignette and rebuild the frame with correct type. Have a native speaker check anything you publish.

## Colour and Texture

| Era | Palette | Hex anchors |
|---|---|---|
| 1894 engraving | Indigo, oxblood, forest, ochre, cream | #1B3A6B, #7B1E2B, #1F5C3A, #C9A227, #F2E8D5 |
| 1909-1935 | French blue, carmine, olive, sepia | #274C77, #A63A44, #6B7A3A, #8A6A4A, #F4EBD9 |
| 1941-1974 | Emerald, vermilion, mustard, sky | #2E6F5E, #C1442E, #C58B2A, #7FA8C9, #F6EFDF |
| Derg | Red, yellow, green, black | #C62828, #F2C230, #2E7D32, #141414, #F5E9D0 |
| Contemporary | Terracotta, teal, saffron, forest, sand | #B85C38, #1F6F78, #E0A526, #2F5D3A, #E8DCC3 |

Rules: two to four inks plus paper; black for text; one gold or metallic accent at most; no neon, no pastel wash, no glossy gradient. Add subtle paper fibre, foxing, or plate wear for historical looks, but stop before it becomes grunge.

**Printing words that steer models.** Historical: `intaglio engraving`, `fine cross-hatching`, `flat ink on cream paper`, `slight misregistration`. Mid-century: `photogravure`, `four-colour lithography`, `soft halftone`. Modern: `offset print`, `flat vector ink`, `crisp typography`, `spot colour`.

## Prompt Recipe

Write the prompt in this order: format, era, exact text, subject, printing, palette, paper and cancellation, negative constraints.

```
A single [vertical / horizontal] Ethiopian postage stamp, [era] style, presented
as a Pinterest pin on cream paper. The stamp has clean perforated edges and a
fine inner frame. Top band: "ኢትዮጵያ". Bottom band: "ETHIOPIA". Denomination
"[value] ብር" at lower left. Year "[year]" at lower right. Central vignette:
[one specific subject in one clear action]. [Printing language and palette].
[Optional circular cancellation "ADDIS ABABA"]. Paper fibre, flat ink, no glossy
3D, no modern poster, no extra text, no gibberish lettering.
```

**1894 first issue add-on.** `intaglio engraving with fine cross-hatching, ornate acanthus border, profile portrait or heraldic lion, deep indigo and oxblood ink on cream, Amharic inscription only`.

**1949 airmail add-on.** `four-colour lithography, oval portrait medallion, globe and cherub motif, "PAR AVION" in small capitals, emerald and vermilion on cream, perforation 14`.

**Derg add-on.** `flat bold poster graphics, red yellow and green fields, black outlines, heroic worker or soldier silhouette, Amharic slogan band, no imperial symbols`.

**Contemporary add-on.** `offset print, flat vector illustration, generous negative space, clean geometric frame, terracotta and teal with gold accent, crisp bilingual typography`.

**Negative prompt.** `glossy 3D, plastic texture, modern poster layout, rounded corners, drop shadow on the stamp, extra text, gibberish lettering, missing perforations, double stamp, sheet edges, watermark, signature, photo watermark`.

## Workflow

1. Choose era, subject, format, and the four text strings.
2. Generate two to four variants at 2:3 with the era add-on. For PixVerse, see `references/prompt-recipes.md`.
3. Inspect each at full size: perforations, frame, top and bottom text, denomination, year, cancellation.
4. Reject without regret if the Amharic is malformed, the perforation is broken, or the stamp looks glossy.
5. Fix the text. Regenerate with fewer words, or crop the vignette and rebuild the frame with correct typography.
6. Compose the pin: stamp centred, cream paper, soft shadow, caption below the stamp. Keep 120 px safe margins.
7. Run the quality checklist.
8. Save the prompt with the image so the result is reproducible.

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] It reads as an Ethiopian stamp, not a generic vintage label.
- [ ] Perforations are complete, regular, and visible at 100%.
- [ ] The Amharic text is correct and legible; nothing is garbled.
- [ ] The Latin text is correct, letterspaced, and inside the frame.
- [ ] The denomination matches the era and currency.
- [ ] The palette and printing language match the chosen era.
- [ ] The subject is specific to Ethiopia, not generic Africa.
- [ ] The Lion of Judah, crosses, and historical figures are treated with dignity.
- [ ] The joke, if any, is affectionate and lives in the subject.
- [ ] The pin is 2:3, with safe margins and no text at the edge.
- [ ] Concept art is labelled as concept art.

## Common Fixes

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Gibberish Amharic | Regenerate with only `ኢትዮጵያ`, or remove the top text and rebuild the frame with a real font |
| No perforations | Add `perforated stamp edge` and `single postage stamp`; use a reference image; add the frame in post |
| Glossy modern look | Add `flat lithographic ink, cream paper, engraved linework, no 3D`; lower the contrast |
| Too much text | Cut to country, denomination, and year. Move the caption to the pin, outside the stamp |
| Generic African motifs | Name the subject precisely: jebena, mesob, walia ibex, gelada, Lalibela, Axum, Lion of Judah |
| Disrespectful humour | Change the subject. Never mock sacred symbols, emperors, or living people |
| Stamp floats in space | Add cream paper, a soft shadow, and an unprinted margin around the perforation |
| Wrong currency | Match the era: guerche before the 1940s, cents in the 1940s-1960s, birr after 1976 |
