# Afewerk Tekle Painting Style

Generate illustrations that capture the monumental, luminous, and culturally resonant painting style of Afewerk Tekle (1932–2012), Ethiopia's most celebrated modern artist and the first African to win the Triennale d'Arte in Rome. His work fuses classical Western realism, dramatic chiaroscuro, and stained-glass cubism with deeply rooted Ethiopian cultural motifs.

## Reference Images

Before generating any prompt, study the reference images:
- `assets/reference-afewerk-tekle-composite.jpg` — 9 paintings showing his range from classical realism to stained-glass cubism
- `assets/reference-afewerk-pinterest.jpg` — Pinterest board showing 24 works spanning his full career

**Key observations from the references:**

### Classical Realism Phase
- Monumental, statuesque figures dominating the frame
- Rich oil painting texture with visible brushwork
- White traditional garments (netela, habesha kemis) glowing against dark backgrounds
- Intricate colorful tilet (woven borders) on white fabric
- Dramatic chiaroscuro — strong light/dark contrast creating deep volume
- Structured backgrounds: architectural pillars, curtains, Ethiopian landscapes
- Jewel-toned colors: emerald, crimson, gold, indigo
- Dignified, heroic posing — never casual

### Stained-Glass Cubism Phase
- Same monumental subjects fractured by intersecting dark geometric lines
- Web of sharp diagonal and vertical lines creating a stained-glass effect
- Colors remain vibrant and jewel-toned within the geometric fragments
- Classical realism underneath the geometric overlay
- Most famous example: the UN Headquarters stained-glass windows in New York

### Two Paths, One Voice
Tekle's style oscillates between or merges these two approaches. When generating, choose one path or combine them — but always maintain his signature luminosity, dignity, and Ethiopian identity.

---

## Style Principles

### 1. Monumental Dignity
Subjects are never casual, never snapshot-like. They are posed, heroic, and project an aura of profound dignity and strength. Every figure looks like they belong on a national monument or in a museum.

### 2. Cultural Synthesis
Seamlessly blend classical volumetric painting techniques (depth, shadow, anatomy, perspective) with deeply rooted Ethiopian identity (traditional garments, artifacts, landscapes, religious objects).

### 3. The Luminous Anchor
White traditional garments — netela and habesha kemis — act as glowing anchors in the composition. They catch the most light and contrast against darker, saturated backgrounds. The white fabric is never flat; it has volumetric folds, soft shadows, and luminous highlights.

### 4. Dual Realities
The style oscillates between smooth classical realism and fractured geometric cubism. In the cubist mode, a web of sharp, intersecting dark lines fractures the composition, creating a stained-glass effect over the figure and background.

### 5. Rich Materiality
The surface should feel like oil on canvas — textured, layered, with visible brushwork. Not smooth digital art, not flat illustration, not photorealism.

## Visual Language

### Anatomy & Proportions
- **Slightly elongated, graceful necks** — a Tekle signature
- **Statuesque posture** — upright, regal, never slouching
- **Sculpted, strong facial features** — defined cheekbones, strong jawlines
- **Deep, soulful, large expressive eyes** — the emotional anchor
- **Elegant hands** — often posed deliberately, holding objects or gesturing

### Skin Tones
- Rendered in rich, warm hues: golden ochre, bronze, deep mahogany
- Soft, glowing highlights on cheekbones, forehead, nose bridge
- Warm shadows that don't go gray — they go deeper brown or amber
- Never flat, never ashen, never cool-toned

### Textiles (Critical Detail)
- **Heavy, volumetric folds** in clothing — the fabric has weight and body
- **Meticulous attention to tilet** — the intricate, colorful geometric woven borders on white Ethiopian garments
- **Tilet colors**: deep red, emerald green, gold, black, blue in geometric patterns
- **The white fabric glows** — it's the brightest element in most compositions
- **Draping is deliberate** — fabric falls in structured, almost architectural folds

### Geometric Intrusions (Cubist Phase)
- A web of sharp, intersecting diagonal and vertical black/dark lines
- Fractures the composition into bold shapes of color
- Creates a stained-glass or subtle cubist effect
- Classical realism remains visible underneath
- Lines are deliberate, not random — they follow the figure's structure

### Contrast Rules
- **High contrast** between glowing white fabrics and deeply saturated dark backgrounds
- **White is the brightest element** — it should almost glow
- **Shadows are warm** — never gray, never blue-black
- **Colors are jewel-toned** — never muted, never pastel, never desaturated

### Forbidden Colors
- Muted or desaturated tones
- Pastel palettes
- Cool gray shadows
- Neon or fluorescent colors
- Flat unshaded color fields

## Lighting

### Chiaroscuro
Strong, dramatic contrast between light and shadow to create deep three-dimensionality and volume. This is the foundation of Tekle's classical approach.

- **Directional light** — from one clear source (upper left or upper right)
- **Deep shadows** — on the opposite side of the light source
- **Modeling through light** — forms are defined by how light falls on them
- **No flat lighting** — every surface has light and shadow

### Ethereal Glow
Light sources often feel slightly theatrical or divine:
- **Soft, luminous glow** on subjects' faces
- **White fabric catches and holds light** — it becomes the light source
- **Background light** — sometimes a soft glow from behind (halo effect)
- **Not realistic lighting** — it's idealized, monumental lighting

---

## Composition Rules

### Heroic Framing
- Subjects dominate the foreground
- Framed from **low or eye-level angle** to emphasize monumental nature
- **Upper body to full body** — never just a headshot (unless cubist portrait)
- **Space around the figure** is structured, not empty

### Structured Backgrounds
Backgrounds are never afterthoughts. They are either:
- **Architectural**: pillars, arches, columns, rich curtains, doorways
- **Landscape**: idealized Ethiopian scenery — lakes, layered mountains, acacia trees, green valleys
- **Interior**: churches, palaces, ceremonial spaces
- **Abstract**: dark, saturated fields of color (for portraits)

### Theatrical Posing
Gestures are deliberate and dramatic:
- A hand gracefully holding a thread or cotton
- Holding a cross or ceremonial object
- Resting thoughtfully on a draped shoulder
- Arm extended in a gesture of presentation or offering
- Hands folded in lap for contemplative subjects

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Making it look like flat traditional art** — Confusing Tekle's volumetric, classical style with the flat, 2D, unshaded style of Ethiopian church murals. Tekle uses perspective, depth, shadow, and volume.
2. **Ignoring the tilet** — Rendering white dresses without the crucial, vibrant woven borders that anchor the Ethiopian identity.
3. **Photorealism** — Making the image look like a modern digital photograph rather than a rich, textured oil painting.
4. **Muted colors** — Using dull, desaturated, or pastel palettes. Tekle's work is renowned for its bold, luminous jewel tones.
5. **Flat lighting** — No chiaroscuro means no volume. Every surface must have light and shadow.
6. **Casual posing** — Subjects should never look relaxed or snapshot-like. Everything is deliberate and monumental.
7. **Missing the glow** — White fabric should be the brightest, most luminous element. If it looks flat white, the prompt failed.
8. **Cool shadows** — Shadows should be warm (deep brown, amber, mahogany), never gray or blue-black.
