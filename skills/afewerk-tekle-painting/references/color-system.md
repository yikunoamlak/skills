# Color System — Afewerk Tekle Painting Style

## Philosophy

Afewerk Tekle's palette is **luminous, saturated, and jewel-toned**. Colors are never muted, never pastel, never desaturated. They glow with inner light, like stained glass or precious stones. The power of his work comes from the richness and intensity of color, combined with dramatic chiaroscuro that makes them vibrate.

---

## Primary Palette

### Titanium White — The Luminous Anchor
- **Hex**: #F8F4E8
- **RGB**: 248, 244, 232
- **Usage**: Traditional garments (netela, habesha kemis), highlights, light source
- **Rules**: This is the BRIGHTEST element. It should almost glow. Never flat — always has volumetric folds with soft shadows and luminous highlights. The white fabric is the compositional anchor.

### Deep Emerald — Life & Ethiopia
- **Hex**: #1B5E20
- **RGB**: 27, 94, 32
- **Usage**: Garments, backgrounds, nature, curtain drapes, stained glass
- **Rules**: Rich and saturated. Use for deep backgrounds, garment details, and landscape elements. Pairs powerfully with gold and crimson.

### Rich Crimson — Power & Sacrifice
- **Hex**: #8B0000
- **RGB**: 139, 0, 0
- **Usage**: Shawls, borders, curtains, lips, ceremonial objects
- **Rules**: Deep, blood-like red. Not bright cherry, not pink. Use for emphasis and emotional weight. Dominant in shawls and religious vestments.

### Majestic Gold — Divinity & Royalty
- **Hex**: #D4A017
- **RGB**: 212, 160, 23
- **Usage**: Tilet borders, crowns, halos, jewelry, embroidery, light
- **Rules**: Warm, luminous gold — not yellow, not metallic. Represents holiness, wealth, and light. Key color in tilet patterns.

### Sapphire Blue — Heaven & Depth
- **Hex**: #1A3A6B
- **RGB**: 26, 58, 107
- **Usage**: Garments, sky, stained glass, shadows, backgrounds
- **Rules**: Deep, royal blue. Use sparingly compared to emerald and crimson. Creates depth and contrast. Key in stained-glass works.

### Warm Bronze — Skin & Light
- **Hex**: #8B6914
- **RGB**: 139, 105, 20
- **Usage**: Skin highlights, architectural details, golden light, wood
- **Rules**: Warm, glowing tone. Use for skin highlights and warm light effects. Bridges between gold and brown.

### Deep Mahogany — Shadow & Definition
- **Hex**: #3E1A00
- **RGB**: 62, 26, 0
- **Usage**: Skin shadows, hair, outlines, deep shadow areas
- **Rules**: The darkest warm tone. Never use pure black for shadows — use this. Creates depth without coldness.

### Royal Purple — Ceremony & Mystery
- **Hex**: #4A0E4E
- **RGB**: 74, 14, 78
- **Usage**: Ceremonial garments, stained glass, accent details (sparingly)
- **Rules**: Use only for specific ceremonial or sacred contexts. Not a dominant color.

### Rich Amber — Warmth & Earth
- **Hex**: #B8860B
- **RGB**: 184, 134, 11
- **Usage**: Warm shadows, wood, earth, architectural details
- **Rules**: Warm, golden-brown. Use for transitional tones between gold and mahogany.

### Forest Green — Nature & Landscape
- **Hex**: #2E5A1C
- **RGB**: 46, 90, 28
- **Usage**: Landscapes, trees, fields, garment details
- **Rules**: Deep, natural green. Use for Ethiopian landscapes and nature elements.

## Tilet (Woven Border) Color System

The tilet is the most culturally significant color element. These geometric borders appear on white garments and carry deep meaning.

### Standard Tilet Palette
| Color | Hex | Pattern Role |
|---|---|---|
| Deep Red | #8B0000 | Primary pattern color |
| Gold | #D4A017 | Secondary pattern, highlights |
| Emerald | #1B5E20 | Accent, geometric shapes |
| Black | #1A1A1A | Outline, definition |
| Sapphire | #1A3A6B | Accent, cool contrast |

### Tilet Patterns
- **Diamond chain**: Repeating diamond shapes in alternating colors
- **Zigzag**: Continuous W or M pattern
- **Cross motif**: Small Ethiopian crosses in sequence
- **Wave**: S-curve continuous line
- **Geometric grid**: Interlocking squares and rectangles

## Color Relationships

### Complementary Power Pairs
- **Crimson + Emerald** — strongest contrast, use for dramatic emphasis
- **Gold + Sapphire** — regal pairing, use for sacred/royal subjects
- **White + Deep Mahogany** — figure-ground contrast, luminous anchor

### Analogous Harmony
- **Gold → Amber → Bronze → Mahogany** — warm skin and light progression
- **Emerald → Forest Green** — nature depth
- **Crimson → Royal Purple** — ceremonial depth

### Neutral Foundation
- **Titanium White + Deep Mahogany** — the core contrast
- **Warm Bronze + Rich Amber** — transitional warmth

## Lighting & Color Interaction

### Chiaroscuro Effect
In chiaroscuro lighting:
- **Lit side**: Colors appear more saturated and luminous
- **Shadow side**: Colors deepen but remain warm (never gray)
- **White fabric**: Brightest where light hits, warm cream in shadow
- **Skin**: Golden highlights, mahogany shadows

### Luminous Glow
- White fabric becomes a light source
- Gold elements catch and reflect light
- Colors in light feel vibrant and alive
- Colors in shadow feel rich and deep

---

## Forbidden Colors

Never use any of these:
- Muted or desaturated tones
- Pastel palettes (light pink, baby blue, mint)
- Cool gray (use warm amber/mahogany instead)
- Neon or fluorescent colors
- Flat unshaded color fields
- Pure black backgrounds (use deep mahogany or emerald)
- Pure white backgrounds (use warm ivory or light amber)

## Color Application by Subject

### Portraits
- Skin: Bronze highlights, mahogany shadows
- White garment: Titanium white with warm shadows
- Tilet: Multicolor geometric patterns
- Background: Deep emerald, sapphire, or crimson

### Landscapes
- Sky: Warm amber to sapphire gradient
- Mountains: Layered greens and blues
- Trees: Forest green with golden highlights
- Ground: Rich amber and mahogany

### Religious
- Vestments: Crimson, sapphire, gold
- Background: Deep emerald curtains, golden light
- Halos: Majestic gold
- Crosses: Gold with red accents

### Cubist/Stained Glass
- All colors remain saturated within geometric fragments
- Dark lines separate color fields
- Light streams through creating glow
- Same palette, fractured into geometric shapes
